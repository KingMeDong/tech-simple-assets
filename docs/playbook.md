# Hook strangers, earn sends, stay original

As of late 2026, Instagram's own statements narrow Reels reach to a short list. Adam Mosseri named **watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach** as the top three signals in January 2025, and said **sends weigh slightly more with non-followers**. For a new account, non-followers are close to the whole audience. His September 2026 series added **engagement rate, originality, recency and "breaking content"** on top: Instagram deliberately tests small creators' posts on people who don't follow them. Distribution works like a ladder. Every eligible Reel goes to a small test audience first and widens if it performs, whatever the follower count. So @tech.simple.explained grows by doing four things: (1) never failing recommendation eligibility (original, no watermark, not "mostly text", 3 minutes or less, no engagement bait); (2) winning the first three seconds, with the sound off; (3) holding watch time through a 35–60 s explainer with a visual change every few seconds; and (4) ending on a payoff people want to send to a friend. Our format of own scripts, code-generated diagrams and new voiceover counts as "original" under Instagram's 2026 definition. Meta's help centre lists "a reel narrated with a realistic AI-generated voiceover" as content that may need a label, and no official source says the label cuts reach, so the AI voice should be disclosed. Captions, hashtags and posting hour matter less. Hashtags are capped at five and "don't work" for reach, and timing studies disagree with each other, so 18:00 German time (12:00 ET / 17:00 UK) is a starting point to test, not a rule. Most numbers about craft, such as skip-rate targets, cut frequency and hook types, come from vendor data or opinion. Treat them as hypotheses to test against our own analytics, which have no per-second retention curve and no follows per Reel. Every rule below carries an evidence tag and a confidence level. The playbook ends with a pre-publish checklist and the experiments that will replace borrowed benchmarks with our own.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **OFF** | Instagram/Meta said it: help, creator or developer pages, newsroom, an SEC filing, or Adam Mosseri. **OFF (press)** = an official statement we could read only through reputable press or trackers. |
| **DATA** | A large-sample study with a stated method: vendor datasets (Buffer, Socialinsider, Metricool, Sprout, Hootsuite, OpusClip, Emplifi) or academic studies. Correlational unless stated. |
| **OPN** | Practitioner opinion, a vendor claim with no method, a single-account anecdote, or an unverified figure. |
| **SETUP** | Our own channel and connector facts (owner decisions, Windsor connector documentation). |
| **→ inf.** | Our inference from the tagged evidence, not a direct statement. |

Confidence: **H** = adopt without testing · **M** = default, verify in analytics · **L** = hypothesis, test before relying on it.

## Watch time, likes and sends per reach decide distribution

Instagram has been unusually explicit about Reels. Its 2023 ranking explainer listed the most important predictions as whether a viewer will **reshare, watch to the end, like, or open the audio page**. It also noted that most of what people see in Reels comes from accounts they don't follow ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). In January 2025 Mosseri boiled this down to "**watch time, likes and sends**". He told creators to watch average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach, and added that likes count slightly more with followers and **sends slightly more with non-followers** ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/)). In 2026 he named engagement rate as the core measure ("of all the people who saw the post, how many of them actually engaged"). He also listed **originality, recency and "breaking content"** as values that sit on top of engagement; breaking content means deliberately testing small creators' public posts on non-followers ([Social Media Today, May 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-engagement-rates-provide-insight-into-reach/821170/); [Parasocial Magazine](https://www.parasocialmagazine.com/news/mosseri-names-four-instagram-ranking-principles-for-creators/)). These statements add to each other rather than conflict, so the January 2025 trio is still the right set of KPIs. What Instagram has *not* said matters just as much. No primary source supports "sends weigh 3–5× likes", fixed watch-time thresholds, or a 2026 update that made skip rate a ranking signal ([Keywords Everywhere tracker](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/instagram-algorithm-updates/)). Meta describes skip rate only as showing "how engaging the beginning of a Reel is" ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/12/03/instragram-api-updates/)). That makes it the hook diagnostic for watch time, not a target in its own right.

## Recommendation eligibility is the gate a new account cannot fail

A new account's reach comes almost entirely from recommendations, so losing eligibility means losing distribution. Instagram defines eligible content as "original, does not violate our community guidelines, has no visible watermarks" and meeting its Recommendation Guidelines. The ineligible categories include **clickbait or engagement bait, misinformation, certain health and finance content, political content, and "low-quality publishing"** ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality)). Instagram also makes Reels less visible when they are **low-resolution, watermarked, muted, bordered, "mostly text", or already posted on Instagram**. Repeated violations can make every post from an account ineligible "for a period of time", and Account Status is where to check and appeal ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). The April 2026 rule defines original content as work "you wholly created or [that] reflects your unique perspective". It counts **new voiceover, unique on-image text and creative graphics that add information** as original. It excludes re-uploads whose only change is a border, a watermark, subtitles or a caption credit. Ineligible accounts recover once a "significant percentage" of their posts in a rolling 30-day window are original ([Instagram Creators, 2026](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram)). Our format clears this bar by design. The real risks are narrower: a borrowed screenshot or product clip, a watermark from an export tool, a Reel that amounts to animated text slides, and sameness across episodes. Instagram has no rule against a consistent series template. Two signals still point the same way, though. Mosseri asks whether you can "make something that only you could create" ([explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/adam-mosseri-ai-negativity-going-to-get-worse-instagram-slop-labels-ranking-what-he-said-2026)). YouTube demonetised "generic, repetitive or template-based" content in July 2026, explicitly including "tutorials that reproduce content already common on the platform" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). Each episode therefore needs its own angle and its own visual idea. Our own logo is fine; logos from other apps are not ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/)).

## The muted first three seconds and a 35–60 second arc make the video

### Open with motion in frame one, explain in three steps, stop at the payoff

Instagram's own guidance says to be "engaging from the first second" and to hook in the first three seconds. It also says Reels should "make sense to people who don't know the creator" ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-reels-creation-tips-in-new-guide/)). There is **no official skip-rate benchmark**. The bands people quote (under 20% strong, 20–30% healthy, over 40% a problem) come from a vendor page that discloses no method ([Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/instagram-reels-skip-rate-benchmarks-2026)). The largest hook dataset is OpusClip's 3.19 million "viral" clips from early 2026. In it, **question/direct-address/intrigue openers (29.9%) and shock/surprise openers (19.4%)** are the most common, while purely visual hooks appear in only **0.04%** of 13.5M clips ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)). These figures show how often each hook appears, not how well each performs. Still, they map neatly onto explainer hooks, and a visual-first opener is cheap for us to make and would stand out. On YouTube Shorts, the same vendor found expertise hooks averaged **about 5.9× the views** of sensational story hooks ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-youtube-shorts)).

The length evidence from 2026 lines up. Buffer compared 2.7M Reels against each account's own baseline. It found **<15 s at −1.5% reach, 30–59 s at +2.7%, 90–179 s at +7.4%, and a −49.3% cliff past 180 s**, and it quotes an in-app alert saying Reels over 3 minutes are not recommended to new audiences ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/)). Socialinsider's 9.8M brand Reels peak at **45–60 s** in both views (10,374) and engagement (0.35%) ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics)). Short clips win on completion percentage, but longer ones win on total watch time, which is the named signal. No study links cut frequency to retention. Practitioners settle on **a visual change every 2–5 s** ([OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention); [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/short-form-video-structure-guide-2026)). Replays count as views ([Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-metrics-to-focus-creators-on-views/723645/)), so a seamless loop is extra upside at almost no cost.

### Burned-in captions, safe zones and a crop-proof cover carry the muted viewer

The sound-off figures people usually quote are old. In 2019, 69% of US adults said they watch video muted in public, and 80% said captions make them more likely to finish a video (Verizon/Publicis, self-reported) ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/)). Skip rate is decided in three seconds, though, so the hook has to work muted regardless. Instagram's guide recommends captions for accessibility even when text overlays are present ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-reels-creation-tips-in-new-guide/)). Alt text cannot be set on Reels through Meta's API ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)) or through Windsor, so burned-in captions are our only accessibility layer. Safe-zone figures are third-party derivations of Meta's spec: about **14% top, 35% bottom and 6% at each side** on 1080×1920, with one source putting the bottom at 40% ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/instagram-reels-safe-zone/); [AdNabu](https://blog.adnabu.com/meta-ads/meta-safe-zones/)). Since January 2025 the profile grid has cropped covers to **3:4**, and the feed can show Reels at 4:5 ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-grid/); [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/guide-to-instagrams-new-vertical-grid); [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-reels/)). The API cannot adjust the grid crop.

Meta's API spec sets the technical limits: MP4/MOV with the moov atom first, H.264 or HEVC, closed GOP, 4:2:0, 23–60 fps, ≤25 Mbps, AAC ≤48 kHz/128 kbps, and **3 s–15 min, ≤300 MB**. `cover_url` takes a public sRGB JPEG of up to 8 MB and overrides `thumb_offset` ([Meta IG User Media](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media)). Windsor allows 1 GB, but Meta's 300 MB is the real ceiling, and our GitHub hosting limit of 100 MB is tighter still. Our target of under 50 MB still leaves about 6 Mbps for a 60 s Reel, which is plenty for flat motion graphics.

## Disclose the AI voice: the label has no known reach cost

Meta requires creators to use its disclosure tool when they post organic content with "photorealistic video or **realistic-sounding audio** that was digitally created or altered", and says it "may apply penalties" if they don't ([Meta SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680124000034/meta-20240418.htm)). The help centre's own example is "**a reel narrated with a realistic AI-generated voiceover**" ([Meta Help Centre](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/), read via search snippets). Our code-generated graphics are not photorealistic, so they don't trigger the rule. The Piper en_US-ryan-high voice plausibly does: it is a neural voice built to sound natural, and whether it counts as "realistic" is Meta's call, not ours. Disclosure costs little. Labelled content stays up. Meta's feed demotion applies to content fact-checkers rate False or Altered. **No official source says an "AI info" label reduces Reels reach** ([Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/)), and third-party guides agree that "the label is not a ranking penalty; unoriginal work is" ([Virallized](https://www.virallized.com/instagram-ai-label)). The only AI-related reach limit Instagram has announced, from 31 August 2026, targets profiles that **feature an AI-generated person** and don't carry the "AI-generated profile" label. It explicitly excludes creators who only use AI tools ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label/)). Our local pipeline (Piper, code rendering, muxing) should not write the C2PA/IPTC markers Meta uses to label content automatically. Any label will most likely come from our own disclosure (→ inf.).

The audience evidence favours honest, high-quality TTS. In a blind 2026 test with 1,326 radio listeners, an AI voice matched a human one on appeal (**61% vs 60%**), and 55% of listeners took it for human. The only significant gap was humour: 26% found the AI spot funny against 33% for the human one. After the reveal, **20% of the AI group felt worse** about the ad and 25% felt better ([Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)). Controlled studies from 2025–2026 find no significant learning difference between human and synthetic voices ([Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14085-y); [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-025-13654-x)). An AI-generated *instructor's face*, however, lowered motivation. An eye-tracking study found that "cute" or formal TTS hurt learning ([Journal of Experimental Education](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00220973.2024.2446169)), and a vendor test found that voice quality mattered more than whether the voice was human or AI ([TechSmith](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/ai-voices-avatars-in-training-videos/)). Adobe's vendor survey is the warning sign: 58% would trust a brand less if its ads used AI voices, and short-form video was among the least accepted uses ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/ai-audio-consumer-trust)). **Our recommendation is to disclose every time, plainly and without apology, and to make accuracy the trust signal.** Windsor's `create_video_post` has no AI-label field, so the caption and bio carry the disclosure, and we add Meta's in-app label afterwards wherever the app allows it. Music follows the same logic as originality. With Instagram Login, Meta's Audio API is unavailable ([Meta Audio API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/content-publishing/audio-api)), so any music is royalty-free and mixed into the file. Our voice track publishes as original audio, and no controlled study shows that background music helps voiced explainers.

## Captions are search snippets and hashtags are only labels

Instagram's search explainer says the typed query "is by far the most important signal". Queries are matched against usernames, profile names, bios, captions, hashtags and places, and Instagram tells creators to put keywords in the caption, not the comments ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works/)). Since 10 July 2025, Google can index public posts from professional accounts owned by users aged 18+ by default ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/instagram-content-becomes-searchable-on-google-starting-july-10/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-indexing-on-google/)), so the first sentence of a caption also serves as a web search snippet. Socialinsider's analysis of 28.6M posts finds Reels engagement at 0.25% for **30–90-word captions**, against 0.20% for shorter or longer ones ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/social-media-caption-length-best-practices/)). Hashtags are capped at **five** ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)). Mosseri says they "don't work" for reach and serve only as labels. Metricool's 24.4M-post study found that posts with any hashtag got 31.7% fewer views ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)). That finding is correlational, but it gives no reason to add more. The Graph API reference still says 30 hashtags, and how the cap is enforced on API captions is undocumented ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media)), which is one more reason to stay at three. For CTAs, Instagram judges intent: engagement bait is when "the end goal is to simply maximize the quantity of engagements". Open questions and genuine conversation are fine ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-single-word-ctas-longer-reels/718151/)). No study measures "send this to…" lines, so each CTA should fit its topic rather than being boilerplate. Caption template:

```
How does AirDrop send files without the internet?
AirDrop uses Bluetooth to find nearby Apple devices, then opens a direct
Wi-Fi link between them, so no router or mobile data is needed.
Send this to the friend who still emails photos to themselves.
Voice: AI text-to-speech. Script written and fact-checked by a human.
#techexplained #airdrop #iphonetips
```

## Every two days at 18:00 German time, weekends included

**Update 2026-11-22:** the owner moved the cadence to one Reel a day (7 per week, Buffer's 6–9 bucket: more reach per post and faster follower growth, as long as quality holds; see T1). The original reasoning for every two days follows. One Reel every two days averages 3.5 per week. That sits inside the 3–5 posts per week that Buffer's within-account study calls the "sweet spot": follower growth there was **more than double** the 1–2-post bucket, reach per post was about 12% higher, and weeks with no posts dragged growth down ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)). Mosseri says ranking keeps feeds from being flooded, so posting often is not penalised, and recency is weighted heavily ([Parasocial Magazine](https://www.parasocialmagazine.com/news/mosseri-names-four-instagram-ranking-principles-for-creators/)). The catch is the calendar. A 48-hour rhythm walks through the week (Tue, Thu, Sat, Mon, Wed, Fri, Sun…), so **3 of every 7 Reels land on Friday to Sunday**. Buffer and Sprout rank those days weakest ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/)). The fix is to keep the rhythm and change only the clock and the content. Hootsuite's data from 118 countries shows weekend peaks around Saturday 11:00 and 17:00 and Sunday 12:00–15:00 local time ([Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)), and our 18:00 German slot lands at 12:00 US Eastern. Most views also arrive within three days ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)), so a Saturday Reel still collects Monday traffic. Because topics are produced in advance, we can send the most shareable topics to Tuesday–Thursday slots and evergreen search topics to weekend slots. The studies disagree on hours: Later even puts the best Reels time at midnight local, using 2024 data ([Later](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)). All of them measure per-follower engagement on established accounts, which matters less for a zero-follower account fed by recommendations. Once we have followers, the in-app "Most active times" replaces these defaults.

## Strangers come first; series, trials and collabs compound later

Instagram says each eligible Reel is first shown to "a small audience of people we think will enjoy it" and is widened "based on its engagement, not just the creator's follower count". It also says "creators of all sizes have an equal chance of breaking through" ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through); [Instagram Creators, Jan 2025](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram?)). An equal chance is not an equal outcome: Metricool found **only 21% of accounts under 10,000 followers grew in 2026** ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)). Reports that new accounts stall near 200 views per Reel are anecdotal ([Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-mosseri-vs-measured)). Our niche shows both how high a faceless channel can go and where there is room. Zack D. Films is faceless and animated, has **11.2M Instagram followers (+207K in 30 days) and posts about 34 times a week** ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/zackdfilms/)). Kurzgesagt posts 3 times a week, mostly cut from long-form, and added 2K followers on 1.6M ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/kurzgesagt/)). The how-it-works channels closest to our topics, such as Branch Education with 2.76M YouTube subscribers, show no sizable Instagram presence in the trackers ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/brancheducation/)). That suggests room on Instagram for short-native, everyday-tech explainers. The top titles in the niche follow a few patterns: curiosity gaps ("How A Kid Redesigned The American Flag", 13.7M views), counterintuitive "Why…" claims, and "The uncomfortable truth about…". For us these become ideas like "Why your Wi-Fi is slower in the kitchen", which are still untested.

Trial Reels show a Reel to non-followers first and report results after about 24 h. They can be shared with followers automatically based on views in the first 72 h ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels)). Windsor exposes `trial_params`: `MANUAL` means graduating the trial in the app, and `SS_PERFORMANCE` means automatic graduation ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)). **Eligibility is contested.** Mosseri's July 2025 expansion, as reported by Social Media Today and BetaNews, opened Trial Reels to **public accounts with at least 1,000 followers** ([Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-expands-access-trial-reels/752895/); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/07/14/instagram-opens-up-trial-reels-feature-to-more-creators/)). Several 2026 guides cite the Help Center as requiring **200 followers for professional accounts** ([Flowgent](https://flowgent.ai/blog/instagram-trial-reels); [Superprofile](https://superprofile.bio/guides/instagram-trial-reels)). Neither research pass could load the Help Center, and no source documents how the API responds for an ineligible account. At launch the conclusion is the same under either threshold: we can't use Trial Reels, and we don't need them yet, because a zero-follower account's normal Reels already go almost entirely to non-followers. They become useful once we have a follower base whose engagement signal is worth protecting. Mosseri says trials "are not going to hurt your account" ([Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/blog/does-scheduling-instagram-posts-hurt-reach/)).

Collabs are the strongest lever for borrowing another account's audience. In Emplifi's H1 2026 brand data, collab posts outperformed standard posts, and Reels benefited most ([Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/instagram-collaborations-boost-organic-engagement/)). Windsor, however, exposes collaborators only for image and carousel posts. Views and follows are only loosely linked: one practitioner reports a Reel with 280,000 views that added almost no followers and one with 4,000 views that added 300 ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/?p=99009)). Advice on running a series is opinion ([Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/?p=275800)), but a named series with a fixed cover template makes the profile grid read as a promise. Stories won't drive discovery at zero followers, and Mosseri says re-sharing posts to Stories doesn't meaningfully change reach ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-debunks-popular-engagement-hack/816781/)).

## Our analytics cover the named signals but not follows or retention curves

The Insights API gives us what we need for the three named signals: reach, views, average and total watch time, likes, comments, saves, shares and reposts per Reel, plus skip rate since December 2025 ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/12/03/instragram-api-updates/)). At account level we get follower counts and non-follower reach. Two things are missing. The first is the per-second retention curve, which Instagram presents as the way to find "the moments in your Reel that keep or lose your viewers' interest" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/)); it is only available in the app. The second is follows per Reel. Since there are no official benchmarks, the baseline is our own rolling median of the last 10–20 Reels ([Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/instagram-reels-skip-rate-benchmarks-2026)). The cadence helps with attribution: with one Reel every 48 hours, the follower change between two publishes mostly reflects the newest Reel, mixed with the three-day tail of the previous one.

## Conclusion

Instagram has removed most of the mystery from Reels ranking. For this channel, the hard part is not decoding the algorithm. It is turning out a stream of genuinely different, accurate, shareable explainers from a code pipeline that could easily repeat itself. That pipeline is our biggest asset and our biggest risk. It makes originality automatic and hook variants almost free, which suits a system that tests every Reel on strangers. It also drifts easily towards animated text slides and sameness from one episode to the next, the two patterns Instagram's rules and YouTube's 2026 policy single out. The AI voice is a disclosure question, not a reach question: the evidence suggests that quality, accuracy and honesty decide trust, and that the label itself costs nothing measurable.

Two implications change how we work. First, timing, hashtags and Trial Reels are minor while the account is small. The time that tuning them would take is better spent on the first three seconds and on a payoff worth sending. Second, the API misses follows per Reel and retention curves, so part of this playbook can't be automated. A weekly in-app review is the only way to see where viewers leave. With n = 1 account and high early variance, the alternating, one-variable experiments below are what will turn this borrowed evidence into our own.

## Pre-publish checklist

Every Reel must pass all items. Rule IDs point to the evidence above.

1. Script, graphics and voice are entirely ours, with no third-party clips, screenshots, product footage or copied UI artwork (E1).
