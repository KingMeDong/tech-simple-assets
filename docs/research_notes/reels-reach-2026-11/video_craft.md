# Video craft for short-form vertical explainers (Instagram Reels first; TikTok/Shorts where it transfers), evidence as of 2026-11-22

Context: a new English-language, faceless channel (@tech.simple.explained) that explains everyday tech with code-generated animation, on-screen text and a TTS voiceover.

Evidence labels used below:
- **[HARD DATA]**: large-sample study with a stated method and sample size.
- **[VENDOR DATA]**: a tool vendor's own dataset. Real numbers, but selection bias and no independent audit.
- **[OFFICIAL]**: platform documentation or a platform statement.
- **[ACADEMIC]**: peer-reviewed or conference research.
- **[OPINION]**: expert or practitioner guidance with no data shown.
- **[UNVERIFIED]**: a number seen only second-hand, or one whose source I could not open.

Research note: creators.instagram.com returned 404, and several Meta help pages came back truncated. Where Instagram's own wording matters, it is quoted from reputable trade press (Social Media Today) or from search-surfaced Meta help-centre text, and flagged as such.

## 1. Hooks and the first 1–3 seconds: what reduces skip rate, which hook types suit explainers, and are there skip-rate benchmarks?

### Takeaway
Instagram measures the opening officially. Since late 2025, Reels Insights show **Skip Rate**: the share of views that skip within the first 3 seconds. There is also a per-second **Retention** chart. There is **no official skip-rate benchmark**, and the published "good skip rate" bands are vendor opinion. The best large-sample hook data (OpusClip, 3.2M clips) shows question / direct-address / intrigue hooks and shock or surprise hooks are the most common openers among viral clips. On YouTube Shorts, expertise and authority hooks beat sensational story hooks by about 6x in views. Both findings suit a "here's how X actually works" explainer. Purely visual hooks are almost never used, which may make them a point of difference.

### Cited Findings
**Platform metrics (what Instagram measures)**
- [OFFICIAL] On 2025-08-24 Instagram announced Skip Rate, defined as "the percentage of views from people who decided to skip your reel during those first 3 seconds". It replaced "view rate" (viewers who watched past 3 s). The same update added a Retention chart: "you'll always see a downward slope, but the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is"; it "shows you the moments in your Reel that keep or lose your viewers' interest." — [Social Media Today, 2025-08-24](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/)
- [OFFICIAL] Meta's developer blog (2025-12-03) says five new metrics were added to the Instagram Insights API, including Reels skip rate. Third-party tools can now pull it. — [Meta for Developers, 2025-12-03](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/12/03/instragram-api-updates/) (seen via search snippet)
- [OFFICIAL, second-hand] Sprout Social's help centre says skip rate applies only to Reels, not to standard posts, carousels or Stories. — [Sprout Social support](https://support.sproutsocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/44454470746253) (search snippet)
- [OFFICIAL, second-hand] In January 2025, Adam Mosseri named **watch time, likes and sends** as the top ranking signals. He told creators to track **average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach**, and said sends matter "slightly" more for reaching non-followers and likes slightly more for followers. — [Zoomsphere, 2026 summary of Mosseri](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach); [Dataslayer, updated June 2026](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)

**Skip-rate "benchmarks" (none are official)**
- [OPINION] Retensis bands: <15% exceptional, 15–20% strong, 20–30% healthy for most content, 30–40% needs work, >40% a problem. It suggests "below 30%" as a realistic target for entertainment Reels. The page **discloses no sample, dates or method**. It recommends benchmarking against "your own median across your last ten to twenty comparable Reels." — [Retensis, published 2026-05-21, updated 2026-09-29](https://retensis.com/blog/instagram-reels-skip-rate-benchmarks-2026)
- [VENDOR DATA, unsourced] OpusClip's Head of Growth says "50–60% of drop-offs occur in the first three seconds" ("data from thousands of Shorts"; dataset not named). He suggests targets of >70% intro retention past 3 s, >60% completion for Shorts under 30 s, and ≥60% average retention. — [OpusClip blog, 2025-11-11](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)

**Hook types: large-sample data**
- [VENDOR DATA] OpusClip classified the hooks of **3,185,590 "viral" clips** (organic only, all platforms, Jan–Mar 2026, published 2026-04-03). Shares of clips by hook type:

  | Hook type | Share |
  |---|---|
  | Direct Address / Intrigue / Question | 29.9% |
  | Shock / Surprise | 19.4% |
  | Intriguing Statement | 10.2% |
  | Question Opener | 8.0% |
  | Story / Anecdote Teaser | 4.6% |
  | Problem / Solution Setup | 3.4% |
  | Personal Anecdote | 3.2% |
  | Bold Opinion | 2.9% |
  | You-Focused Appeal | 2.0% |
  | Process Explainer | 1.8% |

  These are **usage shares among clips the vendor deems viral**, not a controlled performance comparison. "Viral" is not defined, and account size is not controlled. — [OpusClip Research, "How to Make a Viral Short-Form Video (2026 Data)"](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)
- [VENDOR DATA] OpusClip's YouTube Shorts analysis: **Expertise / Authority / Credibility hooks averaged 6,706 views, about 5.9x the 1,132 views of "Outrageous Story Reading" hooks.** This conflicts with its all-platform finding that Direct Address / Intrigue / Question hooks lead. — [OpusClip Research, YouTube Shorts](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-youtube-shorts) (search snippet; methodology not checked)
- [VENDOR DATA] "Visual hooks" appear in only **0.04%** of 13.5M clips (4,806 clips; Jan–Mar 2026). — [OpusClip B-roll & visual effects research](https://www.opus.pro/research/broll-visual-effects-short-form); [OpusClip viral video research](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)
- [OPINION] Third-party commentary notes that these drop-off and retention numbers are vendors analysing their own data, not independent research. — [QuickReel](https://quickreel.io/blog/clip-hook-length-data)

**Hook construction guidance (opinion and platform ad guidance)**
- [OFFICIAL – TikTok ads] TikTok advises introducing the core message "in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness." — [TikTok Ads Help: Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices) (search snippet)
- [OPINION] Show something visual *before* speaking. Open with one specific or surprising sentence: a bold claim, a surprising result or a question. The guide claims viewers decide in about 1.5 s but cites no source. — [Socialync, 2026-03-14](https://www.socialync.io/blog/short-form-video-structure-guide-2026)
- [OPINION] A benefit-stating text overlay on the opening "performs consistently well" (no evidence given). An intriguing opening line beats context or background. — [Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/instagram-reels-skip-rate-benchmarks-2026)

### Inferences
- Explainer hooks for this channel map onto the top OpusClip categories. The "question" hook ("How does AirDrop find a phone with no internet?") is Question / Intrigue. The "surprising fact or number" hook ("Your phone sends AirDrop over Wi-Fi *and* Bluetooth at once") is Shock / Surprise or an Intriguing Statement. An authoritative framing ("Engineers designed AirDrop to…") matches the Expertise / Authority hook that led on Shorts. The "you've been doing X wrong" hook is a You-focused / Bold-opinion variant. It is less common in the data, but people use it.
- An animated pattern interrupt on frame 1 (something moving or transforming before the voice starts), paired with a text hook, is a rarely used combination according to OpusClip's 0.04% figure. It is cheap to produce with code-generated graphics, which makes it a sensible A/B candidate.
- Because skip rate covers only the first 3 s, the hook has to work **muted** (see Section 5). The first frame should carry the question as large text plus a moving visual.
- With no official benchmark, the practical method is to compare skip rate across your own Reels. Retensis's "<30% workable, <20% strong" is only a provisional target.

### Gaps
- No official Instagram skip-rate benchmark exists. No large public dataset of skip rate by hook type was found for Instagram.
- A widely repeated quote, "the first three seconds are super important", attributed to Mosseri in an official Instagram video, could not be verified against the original.
- The claimed Instagram guidance "hook in the first 3 seconds (Creators site) vs 5 seconds (business site)" could not be checked, because creators.instagram.com returned 404.
- A CHI 2024 paper on the first three seconds (Lin et al., doi 10.1145/3613905.3651018) was referenced second-hand but not opened.
- OpusClip's hook data is weighted toward TikTok/YouTube and podcast-style clips. It is not specific to animated explainers.

## 2. Optimal length: data-backed ranges for 2025–2026, completion vs watch time, and the recommended length for explainers

### Takeaway
The two largest 2026 datasets agree that **under 30 s is not the reach sweet spot on Instagram**. Buffer (2.7M Reels, within-account comparison) finds 30–59 s at +2.7% reach and 90–179 s at +7.4%. Socialinsider (9.8M brand Reels) finds views and engagement peak at **45–60 s**. Both show a **cliff past 180 s**, and Instagram's in-app alert says Reels over 3 minutes are not recommended to new audiences. Shorter clips win on completion percentage, while longer ones win on total watch time. For a new explainer channel, **~35–60 s as the core format, with 60–90 s for richer topics**, is the defensible default. Hard cap: 3 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD DATA] **Buffer, 2026-11-12.** 2,730,166 Reels for reach and 2,463,999 for engagement (from 3,780,620 Reels), published via Buffer from 2026-01-01 to 2026-08-15. Each account is compared with **its own baseline**: accounts needed ≥3 Reels in a bucket and ≥2 buckets, reach was log-transformed, and engagement capped at the 99th percentile. Reach vs. account baseline:

  | Length | Reach vs. baseline |
  |---|---|
  | <15 s | −1.5% |
  | 15–29 s | −0.9% |
  | 30–59 s | +2.7% |
  | 60–89 s | +1.6% |
  | 90–179 s | +7.4% (typical account about +4%) |
  | 180 s+ | −49.3% (views −45.9%) |

  Engagement rate was highest for 180 s+ (+2.65 pp vs. baseline; +1.1 pp for the typical account), but those Reels reach about half as many people. Buffer quotes an in-app alert: **Reels longer than 180 s are not recommended to new audiences**, even though up to 20 minutes can be posted. Buffer reports no completion or watch-time data. — [Buffer, "How Long Should Instagram Reels Be? We Analyzed 2.7 Million Reels"](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/)
- [HARD DATA] **Socialinsider, published 2026-08-21, updated 2026-11-03.** 9,790,105 organic Reels from about 297k brand accounts (1K–1M followers), Jan–Jun 2026. Engagement rate = (likes + comments) / followers, so it is **per follower, not per view**:

  | Length | Avg ER | Avg views |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0–30 s | 0.28% | 4,700 |
  | 30–45 s | 0.30% | 8,564 |
  | **45–60 s** | **0.35%** | **10,374** |
  | 60–90 s | 0.30% | 9,790 |
  | 90–120 s | 0.30% | 8,000 |
  | 120–180 s | 0.33% | 9,000 |
  | >180 s | 0.15% | 4,428 |

  — [Socialinsider, Social Media Video Statistics 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics)
- [HARD DATA – TikTok, transfer] Same Socialinsider study for TikTok. Engagement rate by views is highest under 30 s (6.00%). Average views rise with length, from 1,200 (<30 s) to 12,000 (120–180 s). — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics)
- [VENDOR DATA, second-hand] Sociable, 7,200+ Reels: 2–3-minute Reels earned about 65% more comments and about 32% more likes per view than 15–30 s Reels, "with no drop in views". — [Sociable](https://www.sociable.how/blog/best-length-for-instagram-reels-engagement) (search snippet; date and method not verified)
- [VENDOR DATA, small sample, older] A CreatorsJet analysis of 500 viral Reels (Oct 2024–Mar 2025) found 7–15 s Reels had the highest average completion rate and shareability. — cited by [Flowshorts](https://flowshorts.app/blog/instagram-reels-length) (second-hand)
- [OFFICIAL, second-hand] The in-app recording limit for Reels became 3 minutes in January 2025, double the previous 90 s. — [Flowshorts](https://flowshorts.app/blog/instagram-reels-length); see also [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/)
- [OPINION] OpusClip (YouTube Shorts): 15–30 s gets the highest retention, "often exceeding 80%" (unsourced). It suggests **educational content at 35–45 s** and three-step tutorials at 25–40 s (author's experience), and says videos over 45 s show "dramatic drop-offs" (no figures). — [OpusClip blog, 2025-11-11](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- [OPINION] OpusClip's 13.5M-clip research says 15–60 s "tends to perform best" but gives no supporting numbers. — [OpusClip Research](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)
- [OPINION] Socialync's explainer template totals 20–40 s. — [Socialync, 2026-03-14](https://www.socialync.io/blog/short-form-video-structure-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- The datasets conflict only on the surface. Completion percentage (OpusClip, CreatorsJet) favours very short clips. Reach and views (Buffer, Socialinsider) favour 30–60 s and 90–179 s. Mosseri names *watch time*, not completion, as a top signal, so a 50 s Reel watched to 70% (35 s) delivers more watch time than a 12 s Reel watched to 100%. **Length should be as long as the explanation needs, and no longer.**
- Buffer's within-account design is the strongest causal hint available: the same account gets more reach at 30–59 s and 90–179 s than at <30 s. For a new account, where reach to non-followers is everything, this argues against ultra-short 7–15 s explainers as the default.
- Practical plan: core format **35–60 s** (one concept, 3 steps), plus occasional **60–90 s** deep dives. Treat 90–179 s as a test after retention curves look healthy. **Never exceed 180 s** while growing.
- Socialinsider's engagement rate is per follower and covers established brand accounts, so its absolute ER numbers will not transfer to a 0-follower account. The *shape* by length is what transfers.

### Gaps
- No public 2025–2026 dataset reports **completion rate or average watch time by length** for Instagram specifically. Buffer and Socialinsider report reach, views and engagement only.
- No dataset isolates **educational or explainer** Reels by length. Length is confounded with content type in the brand datasets.
- Buffer does not state which engagement-rate formula it uses.
- The "20-minute upload" option is mentioned in Buffer's article but its rollout status was not verified.

## 3. Pacing and editing: cut frequency, visual change every N seconds, motion, saving the payoff, loops and rewatches, open loops

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study links cut frequency to Reels retention. Practitioner guidance clusters around **a visual change every ~2–5 s**. OpusClip says one cut every 2–4 s among high performers, and Socialync says a visual change every 3–5 s with uninterrupted speech under about 8 s. Both are unsourced. Loops matter mechanically, because Instagram's **Views metric counts replays**. The only official instrument is Instagram's per-second **Retention chart**, which should drive pacing fixes post by post.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION / VENDOR, unsourced] High-performing Shorts "average one cut every 2–4 seconds" ("data suggests"; no source named). The same piece suggests speeding up action 1.2–1.5x, says "even a 10% replay rate can significantly boost" distribution (unsourced), and cites one creator whose retention "doubled" after cutting the first five seconds (anecdote). — [OpusClip blog, 2025-11-11](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- [OPINION] Change something visually every 3–5 s. Keep uninterrupted speech under about 8 s. Use mini open loops in the body ("I'll come back to why this matters in a second"). Cut repetition, unnecessary context and tangents. No data cited. — [Socialync, 2026-03-14](https://www.socialync.io/blog/short-form-video-structure-guide-2026)
- [OPINION, unsourced] Other creator guides suggest a cut or visual change every 1.5–3 s and an average shot length of 1.5–2.0 s for TikTok. One claims videos whose "longest unbroken visual stretch was under 3 seconds had 22% higher average retention" without giving a source. — creator guides found in search, e.g. [DEV Community pacing guide](https://dev.to/bulkdl/why-your-video-pacing-is-killing-your-retention-a-shot-by-shot-fix-guide-1ga), [Komet Media](https://www.kometmedia.com/tutorials/pacing-and-cuts-for-high-retention-short-video) (which guide made which claim was not verified)
- [OPINION] Socialinsider recommends "pacing with text or audio shifts" and looping videos so they replay. This is advice, not a finding from its dataset. — [Socialinsider, 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics)
- [VENDOR DATA] Explicit "transitions" were detected in only 2.4% and "B-roll" in 6.0% of OpusClip's 13.5M clips. That dataset leans toward talking-head and podcast clips, so the low rates mostly reflect that format. — [OpusClip Research](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)
- [OFFICIAL, second-hand] Replays count toward Reels Views. Meta replaced "Plays" with a unified "Views" metric across formats, which counts every time a Reel starts playing, replays included. Sprout Social noted in June 2024 that its reporting moved to "total plays", initial plays plus replays. — [Social Media Today on Instagram's Views metric](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-metrics-to-focus-creators-on-views/723645/); [Sprout Social support, June 2024](https://support.sproutsocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/27979378388749-Total-Plays-Now-Included-in-Facebook-and-Instagram-Reels-Reporting-June-2024)
- [OPINION] Loop technique: end with a callback to the opening question so the video replays smoothly, or cut immediately after the payoff. Keep any CTA to 2 s at most. — [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/short-form-video-structure-guide-2026)
- [OFFICIAL] The Retention chart shows "the moments in your Reel that keep or lose your viewers' interest", and "the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is." — [Social Media Today, 2025-08-24](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/)
- [ACADEMIC, second-hand] A 2024 study (Haliti-Sylaj & Sadiku, n=150 college students) linked frequent short-form viewing to reduced sustained attention. This concerns audience attention habits, not editing technique. — cited by [Blaubeck blog](https://blaubeck.com/blogs/product-use/short-form-video-retention-fitness-creators) (original not opened)

### Inferences
- For code-generated animation, "cuts" can be **scene or state changes**: a new diagram element appears, the camera zooms, a colour highlight lands, a counter ticks. A practical rule is **a meaningful visual change every 2–4 s** and no static frame longer than about 4–5 s. Each change should carry information, not just motion.
- **Keep the payoff until the end, but give a partial payoff early.** Answer "what" in the first 10 s and save "the surprising why" for the end. This follows the open-loop guidance and avoids the bait-and-switch feel that drives early exits.
- **Seamless loop for explainers:** make the last frame visually identical or continuous with the first frame. For example, the final shot returns to the opening AirDrop icon with the question text, so an auto-replay looks intentional. Because replays add to Views and likely to watch time, a loop is cheap upside.
- Use the Retention chart for diagnosis. A sharp drop at second N means the visual or script at N failed, so re-pace that beat in the next video.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or large-sample study ties cut frequency or shot length to Instagram retention. All "every N seconds" numbers are practitioner heuristics.
- It is not public whether Instagram's "average watch time" includes replay time, or how strongly replays weigh in ranking.
- No data was found on the effect of motion intensity (e.g., constant camera movement versus static diagrams) in animated explainers.

## 4. Structure templates for explainer shorts (hook → setup → steps → payoff → CTA) and story/retention techniques

### Takeaway
The consistent template is **Hook (0–3 s) → numbered body points that escalate → payoff that keeps the hook's promise → 0–2 s end (cut, tiny CTA, or loop)**. The body is about 70–80% of runtime and the payoff 10–20%. This is practitioner consensus, not tested data. OpusClip's storyline analysis shows **"Expert Explainer" is the most common storyline among viral clips**, and a **serious, thought-provoking tone** is the most common tone, which supports the channel's format.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] Socialync template (20–40 s total):

  | Segment | Time | Content |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hook | 1–3 s | Bold claim, surprising result or question, then a bridge |
  | Body 1 | 5–10 s | Setup, then a transition ("but here's where it gets interesting") |
  | Body 2 | 5–10 s | More value, then a transition ("the part nobody talks about") |
  | Body 3 | 5–10 s | Peak interest, then a transition ("so here's what actually works") |
  | Payoff | 3–5 s | The specific answer the hook promised |
  | End | 0–2 s | Cut, quick CTA or loop setup |

  The guide sets body at 70–80% and payoff at 10–20%, with the same proportions for longer videos. For explainers it recommends a "Linear Build" (each point more interesting than the last). For tutorials it recommends a "Demonstration" structure: preview the result in the hook, speed through dull steps, slow down on interesting ones. It also suggests numbering points ("Step 2 of 3"). No data cited, and the post is promotional. — [Socialync, 2026-03-14](https://www.socialync.io/blog/short-form-video-structure-guide-2026)
- [VENDOR DATA] OpusClip storylines among viral clips (n = 14,213): **Expert Explainer 2,626 (top)**, Confession-style anecdote 2,103, Absurd Q&A sketch 1,392, Sitcom banter 1,165, Numbered-tips 1,102. Tone (n = 10,598): "Serious and professional + Thought-provoking" was the top combination at 25.7%. — [OpusClip Research, 2026-04-03](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)
- [OPINION] OpusClip suggests three-step tutorials (25–40 s) as a "sweet spot" and a new beat every 5–7 s in story formats (author's experience). — [OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- [OFFICIAL, second-hand] Mosseri's emphasis on **sends per reach** (DM shares) as a key signal for reaching non-followers. — [Zoomsphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach)

### Inferences
- A channel template, about 45 s:
  1. **0–2 s:** animated visual plus a text-hook question.
  2. **2–6 s:** stakes or teaser ("It uses two radios at once — here's the trick").
  3. **6–36 s:** three numbered steps, each with one diagram state and one key term, building to the most surprising step.
  4. **36–42 s:** payoff, a one-sentence answer that recaps the diagram.
  5. **42–45 s:** a loop back to the opening frame, or a micro-CTA ("Send this to the friend who always asks").
- Design the payoff to be **shareable**, since sends per reach drives non-follower reach. A crisp, quotable "so that's why…" line or a myth-busting fact gives viewers a reason to DM it.
- Keep CTAs minimal (≤2 s). No source supports long verbal CTAs in short-form, and every extra second adds an exit point at the end of the retention curve.

### Gaps
- No controlled data compares structures (e.g., answer-first vs. answer-last) on Instagram retention or shares.
- No data was found on the effect of a spoken CTA vs. a text CTA vs. no CTA on Reels follows or shares.

## 5. On-screen text and captions: sound-off share, burned-in vs. auto-captions, word-by-word animated captions, text size and safe zones, cover/thumbnail

### Takeaway
The widely cited "most people watch muted" figures are **old (2016–2019) and from Facebook or US ad-supported video**, and no 2025–2026 Instagram-specific sound-off figure was found. Even so, about 80% of viral clips in OpusClip's 13.5M-clip dataset carry captions, and about 79% carry *animated* captions, so burned-in captions are the norm. Keep text inside a centre-safe box. Third-party derivations of Meta's Reels spec give roughly **14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides** clear on 1080×1920 (sources differ on the bottom: 20–40%). Since 2025 the profile grid crops covers to **3:4**.

### Cited Findings
**Sound-off viewing**
- [HARD DATA, 2019, US] Verizon Media + Publicis Media survey of 5,616 US adults aged 18–54: **69% watch video with sound off in public**, and about a quarter do so in private. **80% say they are more likely to finish a video when captions are available** (self-reported intent, not measured completion). — [Forbes, 2019-07-31](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/)
- [UNVERIFIED, old] "85% of Facebook video is watched without sound" traces to Digiday reporting from 2016, not a primary Meta publication. The "92% of mobile viewers watch with sound off" figure is widely repeated from the same 2019 Verizon/Publicis study, but often without the mobile qualifier. — summarised in [Kapwing subtitle statistics](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/subtitle-statistics/) and [Vidmint caption statistics 2026](https://vidmint.io/blog/caption-statistics-2026.html) (second-hand; primary not opened)

**Captions in practice**
- [VENDOR DATA] Among 13.5M+ clips (Jan–Mar 2026), **captions appeared in 80.2%** and **caption animation in 78.6%**. On-screen "text overlay" beyond captions appeared in only 4.5%. Caveat: OpusClip's product auto-generates animated captions, so the rate is inflated by selection. — [OpusClip Research](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)
- [VENDOR, unsourced] "Burned-in captions yield 15–25% higher retention" ("according to performance data"; no source or controls). — [OpusClip blog, 2025-11-11](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- [OPINION] Metricool says the Reels algorithm reads "the audio track used and the keywords in your caption" as signals. Because many users watch muted, it recommends auto-captions or kinetic typography. — [Metricool, Reels algorithm guide](https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-algorithm/)
- [HARD DATA, tangential: post caption, not on-screen text] Metricool's 2026 Instagram study (24.4M posts, 375,118 accounts; Jan–Feb 2025 vs. Jan–Feb 2026) found posts with at least one hashtag had **31.70% fewer views and 33.89% fewer interactions** than the platform average. This is correlational and supports natural-language, keyword-rich captions. — [Metricool press release, 2026](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)

**Safe zones (1080×1920)**
- [OPINION, derived from the Meta Ads Guide] Keep about **14% top (≈270 px), 35% bottom (≈672 px) and 6% each side (≈65 px)** clear, leaving a safe box of about 950×978 px. One guide says Meta's current help page states the rule in words only, and that the percentages are its own arithmetic from Meta's Ads Guide shares. Ads Manager has a "Safe zone" guardrail overlay for checking creatives. — [Lilach Bullock, Instagram Reels safe zone](https://www.lilachbullock.com/instagram-reels-safe-zone/); [Socialync safe-zone tool](https://www.socialync.io/tools/safe-zones/instagram-reels); [Lilach Bullock on Facebook Reels ads](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-reels-ads/)
- [CONFLICT] Another source claims Meta says **40% (768 px)** for the bottom, and others give 20–35% (20% is the Stories figure). — [AdNabu](https://blog.adnabu.com/meta-ads/meta-safe-zones/); [AdsUploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)
- [VENDOR CLAIM] The bottom UI bar grew in a late-2025 update, so older safe-zone guides understate the bottom margin. — [EzUGC](https://www.ezugc.ai/blog/master-instagram-safe-zones) (unverified)

**Cover / thumbnail**
- [OFFICIAL, second-hand] In January 2025 the profile grid moved from square to **3:4**, and every grid thumbnail is cropped to 3:4, which trims the top and bottom of a 9:16 Reel cover. Upload covers at 1080×1920 and keep the key title inside the central 3:4 area. Buffer lists the grid preview as 1080×1440; Planoly says a 1080×1920 cover displays as 1013×1350. The preview can be adjusted after publishing ("Adjust preview"). — [Buffer, Instagram grid](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-grid/); [Planoly vertical grid guide](https://planoly.com/blog/guide-to-instagrams-new-vertical-grid); [Kapwing grid dimensions](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/instagrams-new-grid-layout-size-and-dimensions-2025/)
- [OPINION] Socialinsider recommends thumbnails with a face and a short hook. This is advice, not a finding, and a faceless channel cannot follow the face part. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics)

### Inferences
- **Burn captions in.** You control the font, size, position and word-by-word timing, and the video works muted, which matters most during the 3-second skip window. The channel's text should work at two levels: (a) a **headline text layer** (the hook question or the step title) and (b) **word-by-word or phrase-level animated captions** of the voiceover. Keep both inside the safe box and away from the diagram's focal point.
- Layout rule for 1080×1920: keep text between about y = 270 px and y = 1250 px, and x = 65–1015 px. Leave extra margin on the right edge (the like/comment/share/audio column) and the bottom (username, caption, audio ticker). If unsure, use the more conservative 35–40% bottom figure.
- Make the cover a designed title card (big 3–5-word question plus a simple icon) centred for the 3:4 crop. Without a face, contrast, a single bold object and short text are the remaining levers (inference, not tested).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 Instagram-specific measurement of the share of Reels watched muted was found. TikTok is often described as "sound-on", but no figure could be verified.
- No data compares burned-in captions with Instagram's own auto-generated (viewer-toggled) captions for retention.
- No data was found on **caption size** (font px or % of frame) or on word-by-word vs. phrase-by-phrase captions.
- TikTok's ads guidance reportedly includes a text-display rate ("5–10 words per second" per a search summary). This looks implausibly high and was not verified on the page, so it should not be used.
- Meta's own Reels safe-zone page could not be fetched, so the percentages above are third-party derivations.

## 6. Voiceover: human vs. AI/TTS (perception, retention, engagement), speaking pace (wpm) and background music

### Takeaway
In blind tests, a **high-quality** AI voice performs about on par with a human voice. In a 2026 study of 1,326 radio listeners, appeal was 61% (AI) vs. 60% (human), and 55% mistook the AI voice for a human. Most controlled learning studies (2025–2026) find **no significant difference** in learning between human and synthetic voices. Older or "cute", low-quality TTS does hurt learning, and **voice quality matters more than the human/AI label.** The penalties come from **disclosure and trust** (a minority feels worse on learning a voice is AI) and from **humour**, where humans score higher. **Critical platform point:** Meta says it *may require an "AI info" label* on Reels with **realistic-sounding AI-generated audio**, and its own help-centre example is a reel narrated with a realistic AI voiceover. No research on short-form speaking pace was found, and general comprehension evidence points to about **140–170 wpm**, staying well under 200. No controlled data on background music was found.

### Cited Findings
**Human vs. synthetic voice: perception and trust**
- [HARD DATA, audio not video] Crowd React Media (Harker Bos Group), fielded May–June 2026, **1,326 weekly radio listeners**, blind split test of two spots voiced by a human actor vs. an AI voice. 59% of the human-voice group correctly said "human", and **55% of the AI-voice group thought the AI was human**. Professionalism, authenticity, credibility, energy and likability differed by only a few points, none statistically significant. Overall appeal was **60% human vs. 61% AI**. The only significant gap was humour: 33% (human) vs. 26% (AI) rated the spot funny. After the reveal, 48% of the human group felt more favourable (4% worse), while 25% of the AI group felt more favourable and **20% felt worse**. Asked in general, 47% said AI use makes no difference, 21% would feel more favourable and 33% less favourable. — [Radio Ink, 2026-07-07](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/); [Radio World](https://www.radioworld.com/news-and-business/research-firm-says-radio-audiences-cant-tell-difference-between-real-and-ai-voices)
- [INDUSTRY CLAIM] iHeartMedia's "Guaranteed Human" policy cites internal research that 90% of consumers want media made by real humans. — [Radio Ink, 2026-07-07](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)
- [UNVERIFIED survey] An Audacy survey is widely quoted as finding 55% trust a human voice vs. 23% AI content. It is repeated mainly by voice-actor vendors, and the primary source was not found. — e.g. [The Voice Finder](https://thevoicefinder.com/human-vs-ai-voice-over-real-talent/)

**Human vs. synthetic voice: learning studies**
- [ACADEMIC, 2026] Randomised experiment, **126 undergraduates**, crossing real vs. AI-generated instructor (visual) with human vs. synthetic voice. **Voice type had no direct effect** on perceived human-likeness, motivation or knowledge retention. The *AI-generated instructor* was perceived as less human-like, which reduced motivation and, through it, retention. — [Education and Information Technologies, 2026, doi 10.1007/s10639-026-14085-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14085-y) (abstract via search; full text paywalled)
- [ACADEMIC, 2025] AI vs. human voices and avatars: "no significant differences between the two types of videos in terms of learning outcomes or participants' perceptions." — [Education and Information Technologies 30:22547–22566 (2025), doi 10.1007/s10639-025-13654-x](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-025-13654-x)
- [ACADEMIC, 2024/2025, contrary] An eye-tracking study comparing a human-recorded voice with "formal" and "cute" TTS voices in instructional videos found the **human-recorded voice significantly improved learning performance and attentional engagement**. The authors recommend human-recorded voices. — ["Cute Computer-Synthesized Voice Hinders Learning Performance in Instructional Videos", Journal of Experimental Education 94(4)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00220973.2024.2446169) (abstract via search; full text returned 403; sample size not retrieved)
- [ACADEMIC, 2025] An ICCE 2025 work-in-progress paper found a natural voice still slightly preferred, but **high-quality synthetic voices performed comparably on clarity and acceptability**. — [ICCE 2025 proceedings](https://library.apsce.net/index.php/ICCE/article/view/6068)
- [ACADEMIC, older] An EEG study of AI vs. human newscasts (Chinese journalism) found greater brain-activity responses to the human newscast. — [PMC10701901 (2023)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10701901/). An older study (93 college students) found learners rated human instructor voices above a computer-synthesized voice. It predates modern neural TTS. — [Frontiers in Psychology, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.746857/pdf)
- [VENDOR DATA] TechSmith's study of training videos with different voices found **voice quality mattered more than AI vs. human**: quiz scores rose as voice quality improved and were slightly better with AI voices. It also found many viewers cannot tell a natural AI voice from a human one. Sample size was not retrieved. — [TechSmith blog](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/ai-voices-avatars-in-training-videos/)
- [EXCLUDED] An Alibaba "product insights" page makes YouTube Shorts retention claims for AI voiceovers (e.g., "83% identified AI in under 2 seconds") with no verifiable origin. These are not used. — [Alibaba page](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/ai-generated-voiceovers-for-youtube-shorts-do-they-boost-retention-or-make-viewers-skip-faster.html)

**Platform rules on AI voice (Meta)**
- [OFFICIAL, via search-surfaced help-centre text] Meta "may require an AI label when content has photorealistic video or **realistic-sounding audio** that was digitally created, modified or altered, including with AI". The example given is "**A reel narrated with a realistic AI-generated voiceover**". There "may be penalties" for content shared without a required label. — [Meta Help Centre, "How to identify AI content on Meta products"](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/) (page body could not be fully fetched; wording from search snippets)
- [OFFICIAL] Meta's labelling approach (April 2024, updated) says a "Made with AI" / "AI info" label may be applied automatically when AI is detected, and creators are asked to self-disclose realistic AI audio and video. — [Meta Newsroom, April 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/); [Meta Transparency Center, labeling AI content](https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content); [Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/761121959519495)

**Speaking pace**
- [ACADEMIC, old, ESL] Griffiths (1990) tested listening comprehension at 100, 150 and 200 wpm with second-language learners. Later work treats ≥200 wpm as a significant comprehension risk and 150–170 wpm as acceptable. — [ResearchGate: "Speech Rate and NNS Comprehension"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229734412_Speech_Rate_and_NNS_Comprehension_A_Preliminary_Study_in_Time-Benefit_Analysis)
- [OPINION] Commercial guides put scripted, comprehension-friendly delivery at about 120–160 wpm and "optimal" audio narration around 150–160 wpm. One voiceover studio recommends a much slower 75–90 wpm "information rate" for English scripts, which is an outlier. — [Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/words-per-minute-speaking); [Improve Podcast](https://improvepodcast.com/words-per-minute/); [Bunny Studio](https://bunnystudio.com/blog/voiceover-words-per-minute-choosing-the-ideal-information-rate/) (which guide gives which band comes from a search summary)

**Background music / audio**
- [VENDOR DATA] Music was detected in only 1.9% and voiceover in 1.1% of 13.5M viral clips. These are usage rates in a podcast- and talking-head-heavy dataset, with no performance comparison. — [OpusClip Research](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)
- [OPINION] Metricool: trending audio aids discoverability (no lift figure published), trending sounds' lifespan is "shorter than ever", and the algorithm begins to favour original audio once a sound is saturated. The audio track is read as a ranking signal. — [Metricool, trending audio](https://metricool.com/trending-audio-on-instagram/); [Metricool, Reels algorithm](https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-algorithm/)

### Inferences
- **TTS is viable** if the voice is high quality (modern neural TTS, natural prosody, correct emphasis) and the script avoids jokes, where humans keep an edge. The 2024/25 negative result involved formal or "cute" TTS, so voice selection and prosody tuning are the main quality levers.
- **Plan for the AI-voice label.** A realistic TTS narrator falls under Meta's own example of content that may require an "AI info" label. Failing to label risks penalties, and auto-detection may label it anyway. Self-label consistently and describe the voice honestly in the bio, e.g. "Narration: AI voice". About 20% of listeners feel worse on discovering an undisclosed AI voice (Crowd React), so being upfront limits that risk. The label's effect on reach is unknown (see Gaps).
- **Avoid an uncanny AI human avatar.** The 2026 study found the AI *instructor's appearance*, not the voice, hurt motivation and retention. Abstract code-generated animation sidesteps this.
- **Pace:** target about **150–170 wpm** for a 45 s Reel (about 110–130 words), with short sentences and brief pauses at step transitions. Short-form audiences tolerate brisk delivery, but staying under about 180–190 wpm protects comprehension for non-native English viewers. Pair this with burned-in captions.
- **Music:** if used, keep it clearly subordinate to the voice, so captions and narration stay intelligible, and choose royalty-free or Instagram-library tracks. Original audio (the TTS track itself) may become a reusable "original audio" asset. Evidence here is weak.

### Gaps
- No controlled study measures **Reels or Shorts retention or engagement for TTS vs. human voiceover**. All voice evidence comes from radio, e-learning or surveys.
- No public evidence on whether Meta's "AI info" label **reduces distribution** of Reels. Meta's pages describe penalties for *not* labelling, not reach effects of labelling.
- No research found on optimal **wpm for short-form social video** specifically.
- No controlled data on **background music** (presence, volume, trending vs. royalty-free) under explainer voiceovers on Reels. No sourced mixing level (music dB under voice) for short-form was found. Instagram's music-library licensing limits for business vs. creator accounts were not researched here.

## 7. Faceless and animated explainer formats: what works without a face on camera?

### Takeaway
There is **no controlled platform data** comparing faceless animated explainers with talking-head Reels. The available evidence is indirect. "Expert Explainer" is the most common storyline among viral clips, serious and thought-provoking is the top tone, and third-person or visual delivery is common, so the *content archetype* fits. Learning research suggests the **voice and visual quality**, not the presence of a human face, drive comprehension, and **AI-generated human avatars can backfire**. Format claims either way are vendor anecdotes.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR DATA] Among viral clips, camera perspective was third-person 77.8%, first-person 16.8% and second-person 5.3% (13.5M+ clips). Delivery style was Conversational 59.4%, Podcast 29.6% and **Visual 11.0%** (multi-label). The Expert Explainer storyline was the most common (2,626 of 14,213). — [OpusClip Research, 2026-04-03](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video)
- [ACADEMIC, 2026] AI-generated (synthetic) instructors were perceived as less human-like, which lowered motivation and indirectly retention. Voice type had no direct effect (n = 126). — [Education and Information Technologies, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14085-y)
- [ACADEMIC, 2025] No significant difference in learning outcomes or perceptions between AI and human voice/avatar videos. — [Education and Information Technologies, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-025-13654-x)
- [VENDOR ANECDOTE] A SaaS video agency reports a 77% watch-through on a 60 s talking-head clip vs. 30% on an expensive animated explainer. This is a single B2B comparison, not a study. — [Komet Media](https://www.kometmedia.com/blogs/talking-head-vs-animated-video-for-saas)
- [OPINION] Talking-head videos often score higher on viewer satisfaction, while animation can aid recall of factual or abstract content in some settings. — [Educational Voice](https://educationalvoice.co.uk/animated-video-vs-talking-head-video-2/)
- [VENDOR CLAIM] Talking-head AI avatars are claimed to get 20–30% higher engagement, backed only by a "27M views" anecdote. — [AI Content Agency community post](https://ai-content.agency/community/ai-avatar-vs-faceless-graphics-animated-character-comparison.html)
- [OPINION] Show something visual before speaking. For demonstrations, preview the end result in the hook. — [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/short-form-video-structure-guide-2026)
- [OFFICIAL, via search snippet] Meta's AI-label rule targets *photorealistic* video and *realistic-sounding* audio. Non-photorealistic, animated or cartoon-style visuals are not the trigger. — [Meta Help Centre](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/)

### Inferences
- Without a face, the **visual has to do the face's job** of grabbing attention and signalling emotion. Use a strong focal object in motion on frame 1, expressive motion design (squash and stretch, highlights, arrows that "point"), a consistent mascot-like icon or colour system for brand recognition, and on-screen text as the "voice" for muted viewers.
- Abstract, non-photorealistic graphics avoid the "AI instructor" penalty seen in the 2026 study, and they are not what triggers Meta's AI label (the TTS voice may be). Do **not** add a synthetic human avatar to "add a face".
- Faceless channels lose the "face on the thumbnail" lever (Socialinsider advice). Compensate with high-contrast title-card covers and a consistent visual template, so the grid reads as a series.
- Make the content itself the trust signal: precise, verifiable explanations, the "Expert Explainer" storyline and a serious, thought-provoking tone, all of which are common in the viral sample.

### Gaps
- No large-sample or platform-published comparison of faceless or animated vs. on-camera Reels (reach, retention, follows) was found.
- No data was found on whether Instagram's ranking treats animated or motion-graphics content differently from camera footage.
- Reports that Instagram stopped recommending accounts that mainly repost others' content (reportedly from 2026-04-30) appeared only in secondary blogs and were not verified. Original code-generated animation should be safe under any originality rule, but this was not confirmed against Instagram's own posts.
